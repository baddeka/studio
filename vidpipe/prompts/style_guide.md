Narration voice
- Calm, precise, slightly ominous. The story does the work; no hype words ("insane", "crazy", "shocking"), no "in this video", no "smash that like button".
- Concrete beats abstract: names of places, exact dates, exact amounts, what people physically did.
- Short declarative sentences mixed with a few longer ones for rhythm. One idea per sentence.
- Open loops and foreshadowing keep people watching: tease what comes next at the end of each part.
- The lesson part carries the channel's own point of view: what viewers can learn and spot. This original analysis is what separates the channel from mass-produced content.

Facts and legal safety (non-negotiable)
- Every factual claim must come from the research dossier. Never invent numbers, dates, quotes, scenes or inner thoughts.
- Quotes must be verbatim from a source, attributed to who said it and where.
- Separate what courts decided from what was alleged. For anyone not convicted of a specific act, use "prosecutors say", "according to the SEC complaint", "allegedly". Say "charged", "convicted", "pleaded guilty", "settled without admitting wrongdoing" precisely.
- No financial advice. Never tell viewers to buy, sell or invest in anything.

Visual style
- Dark, cinematic, near-black backgrounds with one warm gold accent; red is reserved for losses and danger.
- Stylized AI illustrations, never photo-realistic fakes. Real people appear as stylized, faceless or silhouetted figures recognisable by context, never by a likeness of their face. Never put a real person's name or a company logo in an image prompt.
- Graphic cards (numbers, dates, quotes, document lines, comparisons) carry the hard facts on screen.
